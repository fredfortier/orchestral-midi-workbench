# Composition stories for the production skills

**Product objective.** Help a composer develop an orchestral performance they can hear, edit, and direct in Bitwig. The work starts from a score, an existing arrangement, or a selected musical figure. The strongest recurring interaction is to identify the musical problem, play a candidate in context, apply the accepted idea to the intended occurrences, and verify the result. The [Couperin audition and repair session, records 880–1439](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/22/rollout-2026-11-22T08-05-43-01a20401-9a0f-7d23-a129-ef609385bebb.jsonl) shows that loop from selection through saved revision.

## Evidence and scope

This review covers locally persisted Codex sessions dated October 21 through November 22, 2026, Pacific time. It includes 157 rollout files whose recorded working directories were the current repository, the retired `MIDI2.0Workbench` checkout, or its `crates` directory. The query returned 9,082 records. Two empty files had unknown project scope and one record in a matching rollout was unreadable. Session links point to original local JSONL or compressed JSONL files. Record numbers refer to JSONL lines after decompression. These links are for local research and require the original Codex archive.

User instructions establish intent for the task in which they were given. Assistant reports, tool output, and user feedback establish different kinds of evidence about delivery. The [current production workflow](../skills/WORKFLOW.md#meaning-and-authority) governs today's skill boundaries. Suggested changes below are product proposals, and historical outcomes do not certify current project state.

## 1. Turn a score into a playable ensemble

**Composer story.** I want to open an editable Bitwig project, recognize its voices and articulations, and hear the selected instruments perform it.

**Delivery and critique.** In Bach BWV 248/64, the user found patch assignments that appeared arbitrary despite a functioning host project. They specified a playable MIDI rendition with explicit companion bindings, readable composer notes, and musical names in Bitwig. The rebuilt project reported 3,984 notes, 22 instruments, 15 successful renders, and a cold reopen. Human listening remained pending. A later pass repaired 98 display names. In Fauré's *Sanctus*, the AI presented an empty saved workspace after spending time on bootstrap work and an MCP dependency. It later verified 3,056 notes, but its own diagnosis described an importer with 450 native steps that repeatedly read the project and recompiled the plan. The later harp session verified both assigned channels and an isolated render. Full ensemble completion remains unverified in the reviewed sessions. ## 2. Direct a performance from its musical structure

**Composer story.** I want the piece's foreground, phrasing, and climax hierarchy to guide the tempo, dynamics, and mix. I want to hear the decisions at the passages where they matter.

**Delivery and critique.** For Vivaldi's *Et in terra pax*, the user delegated a Corboz-inspired interpretation on modern instruments. The AI chose tempo and continuo instrumentation, placed 14 cue markers, and used the alto suspension at bar 65 as the principal harmonic climax. The later mix brought the alto forward and reduced a late cello surge. Nine reference recordings were collected. When the user reported crackling, the AI ran several buffer, sample-rate, and routing comparisons. One recorder had captured the wrong source, and subsequent clean diagnostics did not reproduce the symptom. The export chooser stall and the audible crackle had different evidence and different outcomes. [Vivaldi direction, records 13–8013](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/03/rollout-2026-11-03T12-19-34-01a1a311-2efb-76c1-b2b4-49613c943081.jsonl.zst) · [Cue placement, record 1900](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/14/rollout-2026-11-14T14-26-57-01a1dc2b-bf81-7040-9d37-68bc26bde9a6.jsonl) · [Mix revision, records 13–443](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/18/rollout-2026-11-18T12-40-02-01a1f063-4f3c-7513-9917-e1eaf3c3efd7.jsonl) · [Reference recordings, record 487](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/21/rollout-2026-11-21T12-16-06-01a1ffc0-79a0-7d80-81ab-6d060728900c.jsonl) · [Playback and export diagnosis, records 260–2149](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/04/rollout-2026-11-04T17-10-52-01a1a942-3b28-7773-9fa7-8dbb67a7844b.jsonl.zst)

**Skill implication.** [Performance cues](../skills/performance-cues/SKILL.md) already ties interpretation to native locations, and [mixing](../skills/bitwig-mixing/SKILL.md) already uses foreground and rendered audio. Make their handoff use the same passage, named musical relationship, and listening question. For a reported playback fault, establish the capture source and a short reproducible passage before changing settings. Report audible approval, render measurements, and live playback faults on their own evidence.

## 3. Make the choir sing intelligible, directed phrases

**Composer story.** I want lyric underlay to become reusable, singable phrases. Each voice should shape its own entry, stress, and resolution.

**Delivery and critique.** Vivaldi began with fixed open-vowel patches. The user corrected the AI's vague routing proposal by requiring one configured phrase per channel, source-lyric names, and evidence for where a phrase can restart. The AI verified 17 configured phrases, 490 vocal notes, and 60 resets after saving and reopening. In Bach BWV 232, a later automation audit exposed missing CC1 curves even though the patch documentation already described modwheel dynamics. The build preserved earlier automation without checking whether it drove the replacement patches. The repair saved six independent vocal-intensity contours. This was a missing handoff between loaded patch, control law, and musical phrase plan. [Vivaldi lyric design and saved verification, records 240–330, 760–836, 6742–7208](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/18/rollout-2026-11-18T13-39-15-01a1f099-8676-7710-b58d-123585cb9195.jsonl) · [Bach CC1 diagnosis and repair, records 13–448](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/19/rollout-2026-11-19T17-01-13-01a1f678-cab4-7a60-bad2-eab2f56c41ee.jsonl)

**Skill implication.** [Syllabuilder](../skills/kontakt-syllable-builder/SKILL.md) already covers source association, reusable phrases, reset and hold behavior, replacement-patch dynamics, and representative audition. [The shared expression convention](../skills/WORKFLOW.md#expression-control-specification) assigns track-level controls separately from note-channel routing. Put the existing checks at the point of handoff: each voice should carry its lyric coverage, named receiver, verified reset/hold behavior, and required controls into editing. Resume with the first missing item when a voice is partially complete.

## 4. Compose a new part that belongs in the piece

**Composer story.** I want an accompaniment or countervoice with a recognizable musical idea, developed in relation to the existing harmony and form.

**Delivery and critique.** For *Peace in Suspension*, the user wanted hypnotic repetition, small variations, smooth chromatic motion between peaks, and very fast fills at the peaks. The first 346-note rewrite passed native readback but failed the brief in the user's judgment. The 1,085-note revision developed the opening violin's B–F♯–D cell, increased the speed into the peaks, and dropped to a low F♯ under the alto suspension. The clip was verified live and soloed, leaving ensemble balance to be judged. An electronic continuo experiment used percussion fills and breaks to serve the same expressive arc. The musical revision was productive. There is no recorded review of a representative passage with the user before the full first draft was applied. A short sample of a connective phrase and a climactic fill would have made the proposed contrast easier to judge. [Peace in Suspension brief, rejection, and revision, records 76–529](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/21/rollout-2026-11-21T13-15-07-01a1fff6-7fd2-7f91-b7ad-3c1fca62ccaf.jsonl) · [Electronic continuo study and edit, records 243–705](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/17/rollout-2026-11-17T17-34-41-01a1ec4a-b497-72b1-9a11-7f455b2cccb7.jsonl)

**Skill implication.** [Score analysis](../skills/score-analysis/SKILL.md), [performance cues](../skills/performance-cues/SKILL.md), and [Bitwig editing](../skills/bitwig-edit/SKILL.md) cover analysis, interpretive anchors, and scoped writes. Their top-level guidance could expose a composition route that connects those capabilities. Resolve the donor material and target, state what the new part does between peaks and at the main arrival, then place a representative phrase or deliver the full revision when requested. Keep precedent, editorial invention, and audible acceptance legible in the project notes.

## 5. Audition a local repair and apply it to matching passages

**Composer story.** I want to point to a figure, hear a repaired version in a Launcher slot, approve it, and apply the musical idea to equivalent Arranger passages.

**Delivery and critique.** In Couperin's *Première Courante*, the user heard a trill that sounded too measured. They approved the quicker Launcher prototype after comparing it with recordings. The AI saved repairs to all 26 marked trill occurrences and restored six written notes that the converter expanded into alternations from wavy lines without explicit trill marks. It filed the converter issue. In Vivaldi, the AI earlier wrote variants to an Arranger clip when the user meant Launcher Scene 2, then undid the edit. It subsequently created the correct Launcher prototype and applied five selected Couperin-inspired figures in the Arranger on channel 2. Those later Vivaldi changes were reported live and unsaved. [Couperin audition, approval, audit, and save, records 880–1439](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/22/rollout-2026-11-22T08-05-43-01a20401-9a0f-7d23-a129-ef609385bebb.jsonl) · [Vivaldi wrong target, Undo, and subsequent edits, records 912–1308](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/21/rollout-2026-11-21T14-26-55-01a20038-3ce4-7443-b9b8-b5ff3d29fdec.jsonl)

**Skill implication.** [Native navigation](../skills/bitwig-explore/references/arranger-navigation.md) distinguishes selection membership, editor and object type. [Bitwig editing](../skills/bitwig-edit/SKILL.md) now has a Launcher route. Make the common request explicit in those entry points: resolve the selected notes and destination surface, prototype without changing the Arranger, then apply to the user-approved class of occurrences. Compare the class with the source score where available. An exact pitch match alone would have missed transposed, varied, and repeated trills in Couperin.

## 6. Extend the piece using the ensemble that is already there

**Composer story.** I want to append a passage to my existing tracks and instruments, keep the preceding performance intact, and hear the join.

**Delivery and critique.** Bach's *Pleni* added 6,341 notes on 20 existing tracks with six fixed-vowel receivers, 37 curves, 13 cues, and verified non-silent choir stems. The Vivaldi *Laudamus te* session found the compatible score and isolated the string ritornello. Tool records show a replacement result with 226 notes on four existing tracks. The AI tested clip placement and 2/4 in a disposable project but had not verified the tempo step when it began the live append. The session ended during repeated tempo-point positioning attempts, before final transition verification or save. [Pleni request and saved result, records 13–1191](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/19/rollout-2026-11-19T18-05-47-01a1f6b3-e7d9-7900-a10d-e37b136bf10b.jsonl) · [Laudamus selection, live replacement and tempo attempts, records 342–878](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/20/rollout-2026-11-20T16-54-01-01a1fb98-8f82-7dd1-bf6e-59b0a3f094e2.jsonl)

**Skill implication.** [Native score import](../skills/score-realization/references/native-import.md) covers owned note placement and preservation, and [keyboard recipes](../skills/bitwig-explore/references/keyboard-workflow.md) cover qualified host actions. The reviewed routes do not give this request an end-to-end transition recipe. Qualify placement, meter, tempo, and save for the exact boundary before writing to the live project. Verify the preceding closing bars, the join, and the first new phrase. Surface any unavailable operation before presenting the append as playable.

## 7. Read back and analyze the arrangement I have now

**Composer story.** I want to analyze the current performance, including my edits, through a fresh score representation.

**Delivery and critique.** Vivaldi's reverse workflow produced a validated nine-part score from native Bitwig data. A timed repeat took 1 minute 49 seconds wall time with 11 seconds in commands and about 55 seconds reported for inspection and interpretation. In a later edit, a CLAP device caused full-scope capture to fail. The AI attempted to combine partial output with an archived staff. The user rejected that substitution. The adapter was repaired and fresh capture took 1.00 second. Conversion alone took 2.25 seconds, so the complete path exceeded the user's two-second target for that run. [Vivaldi export, timing and analysis handoff, records 1706–2212](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/19/rollout-2026-11-19T08-26-36-01a1f4a1-a5de-7d61-830b-aeef334be04d.jsonl) · [Capture failure, correction, and timing, records 247–713](../../../../.codex/sessions/2026/11/21/rollout-2026-11-21T14-26-55-01a20038-3ce4-7443-b9b8-b5ff3d29fdec.jsonl)

**Skill implication.** [MIDI2 export](../skills/bitwig-export-midi2/SKILL.md#required-capture-for-the-requested-scope) now requires successful exact-scope capture and uses direct assembly by default. [Score analysis](../skills/score-analysis/SKILL.md) already calls that route for a fresh DAW score. Preserve this behavior and test the routine path with one capture, targeted inspection, and timings measured separately for tool execution and editorial decisions. Keep the live-clip reading route available for questions answered directly from the selected notes.
