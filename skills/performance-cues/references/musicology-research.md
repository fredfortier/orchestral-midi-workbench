# Research decisions that affect the performance

Read when establishing or disputing musical function, style or interpretive
priority. Begin with questions from the native assessment, not a biography survey.
Output is a short, cited justification for particular cue decisions, with
uncertainties carried into the performance file.

## Find and evaluate evidence

Identify composer, title/catalogue number, movement, text, edition and arrangement.
Search the actual piece and process: for example `Vivaldi RV 589 Et in terra pax
autograph suspension`, rather than generic “Baroque emotion”. Search musicology
journals, thematic catalogues, critical edition prefaces/commentaries, manuscript
studies and scholarly monographs; use institutional repositories, publisher
sites and scholarly indexes to locate them. Performance-practice treatises and
instrument manuals answer different questions from work-specific analysis.

Read the relevant pages/examples, including their context. A search snippet,
abstract, inaccessible PDF or citation in another source does not establish its
contents. Prefer primary research and critical sources; identify programme-note
interpretations as such. Cite author, title, page/example or section, URL and
the edition/revision when consequential. Keep quotations brief; paraphrase with
accurate attribution. Record inaccessible evidence as unverified and seek an
accessible authoritative source rather than citing imagined support.

Cross-reference each consequential claim against the current score/notes:

| Evidence | What it establishes | Performance consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manuscript/critical commentary | Authored alternatives, dissonances, editorial signs | Prioritise a voice or distinguish notation from an editorial choice |
| Work-specific harmonic/contrapuntal study | A supported interpretation of a passage | Shape preparation, arrival, resolution and relative climax weight |
| Historical performance-practice source | A contextual practice, not an automatic instruction for every work | Articulation, pulse, ornamentation or agogics, qualified for the forces/edition |
| Native capture/score | Actual pitches, timing, texture and notation in this rendition | Confirm the passage and feasibility; expose changes from the cited edition |

Bar numbering may differ through pickups, cuts, editions or arrangements. Locate
the described musical event by voice-leading, thematic/text entry and surrounding
cadence before attaching its claim to a cue key. Do not transpose a source's bar
number directly into the DAW. Cite conflicting readings and explain the chosen
performance interpretation; do not claim scholarly consensus from one author.

## Keep research bounded and useful

Prioritise the principal climax, subordinate arrivals, phrase/entry structure,
cadential behaviour, historically meaningful articulation and text rhetoric.
Each retained finding must change or justify an actual cue action. Omit facts
that do not help a performer decide what to do. Affective readings such as
“supplication” need musical mechanisms—suspensions, independent counterpoint,
harmonic instability—not unsupported psychological narration.

The request's RV 589 II sources are research leads, to inspect before reuse:

- Michael Talbot, [Hyperion movement note](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dw.asp?dc=W2906_66849): rhetorical/affective context.
- [Everett, *Vivaldi at Work: the Autograph of the Gloria RV 589*](https://www.academia.edu/115779835/Vivaldi_at_Work_the_Autograph_of_the_Gloria_RV_589), Examples 8–9: proposed harmonic culmination.
- [Pysh, Choral Journal, April 2011](https://acda-publications.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/choral_journals/CJApr11.pdf), printed p.34: vocal and string texture.
- [Bruno–Ritchie, Bärenreiter preface](https://www.barenreiter.co.uk/prefaces/9790006538843_Innenansicht.pdf), “Fermate”: distinguish cadential punctuation from an editorial extended final hold.

Do not hard-code these readings for another work, nor repeat the example's pitch,
bar or suspension claims without checking. Stop when the performance decisions
are defensible; report unresolved material questions instead of collecting more
unrelated sources.
